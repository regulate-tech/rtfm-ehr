A Happy Half Hour on Electronic Health Records

Ever had to make a decision about a multi-million pound NHS IT project? Ever wondered what an 'Electronic Health Record' system actually is? This tutorial is for you.

In just 30 minutes, this hands-on guide demystifies health tech. You will run a simple, safe 'toy' version of a health record system right on your own computer. You'll learn what it means to 'run code,' see how patient data is handled, and gain the confidence to ask better questions in your next policy or procurement meeting. No technical background required.

Try It Yourself: See How the Data Flows

This page is your "command centre." We'll "role-play" as different staff members to see how data moves through a modern health system.

You'll use the navigation bar at the top of this page (e.g., "Admin", "Data Entry") to perform an action, and then return here for the next step. Let's begin!

Role 1: The Administrator

Let's start by getting the system ready. We have 20 patients in our main "customer list" (the PMI), but they don't have "secure medical files" (the EHR) yet. Let's create them.

  1. In the top navigation bar, click on "Admin".
  2. Click the button that says "Sync All PMI to EHR".
  3. You should see a success message.
  4. Now, come back to this page for the next role.

What just happened?

You just told the system: "Please read all 20 names from the 'Customer List' and create 20 new, empty, secure medical files, one for each of their NHS numbers."

Our 20 patients are now "in the system," ready to have clinical data added.

Role 2: The Data Entry Clerk

Now, let's pretend a patient has had their blood pressure taken and a lab test done.

Part A: Get a Patient ID

  1. Go back to the "Admin" page in the top navigation bar.
  2. Click on "Search & Delete Patient Master Index Records".
  3. Search for any term, eg the letter 'a' and this will show patient records.
  4. Copy the NHS number of at least one of the patients in the list (e.g., 99999901). You'll need this for the next few steps.

Part B: Enter Clinical Data (Two Ways)

  1. Now, in the top navigation bar, click on "Data Entry".
  2. Paste the NHS number (99999901) into the search box and click "Search Patient".
  3. You'll see the patient's details and two new buttons. Let's try the first one, just like a nurse would.
  4. Click "Enter Clinic Check Vitals".
  5. Enter a Systolic value (e.g., 120) and a Diastolic value (e.g., 80). Click "Submit Vitals".
  6. You'll be back on the patient's data entry page. Now let's simulate an automated lab result.
  7. Click the "Generate Random HbA1c Result (30-50mmol)" button.
  8. You should see a success message for both entries.
  9. Great! Return to this page for the next role.

What just happened?

You just submitted clinical data in two different ways!

  • First, you manually entered data (blood pressure) using a standard form, just like a nurse.
  • Second, you triggered an automated data feed (the lab result), just like a lab machine would.

Both sets of data were securely filed in patient 99999901's "Secure Medical File."

Role 3: The Clinician

Time to be the doctor. Let's find that patient's history.

  1. In the top navigation bar, click on "Clinician View".
  2. Paste the same NHS number (99999901) and click "Search Patient".
  3. You will now see two "compositions" listed: the "Clinic Check" (blood pressure) and the "Lab Result" (HbA1c).
  4. All done! Come back to this page for the final role.

What just happened?

As the 'clinician', you've just looked into the patient's secure file and can see all the data, from all the different sources, in one place.

Role 4: The Patient (The 'Patient Portal')

Finally, let's see this from the most important perspective: the patient.

  1. In the top navigation bar, click on "Patient View".
  2. Paste the same NHS number (99999901) and click "Search by NHS No".
  3. Look closely at what you see.
  4. Congratulations! You've finished the tour.

What just happened? (This is the "Aha!" Moment)

You've just seen a "Patient Portal" in action. Notice that it pulled information from two completely separate systems at the same time:

  • Demographic Details: Their name and address were pulled from the "Customer List" (the PMI).
  • Clinical Data: Their blood pressure and lab results were pulled from the "Secure Warehouse" (the EHR).

This is the core concept of modern, connected IT: a single, simple application can securely combine data from multiple, specialized databases to give the user (whether a doctor or a patient) a single, complete picture.
